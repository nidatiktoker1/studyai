Cornell Notes Format: The Exact Layout

StudyAI Guides · Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

Formats exist so you stop deciding where things go and start listening. The Cornell format divides one page into four parts that never move: a header line, a narrow cue column on the left, a wide notes area, and a summary box across the bottom. This page gives the layout with real measurements — for notebook paper, for Word and Google Docs, and for the printable template. For what the method is, start with what are Cornell notes; for the routine, see how to take Cornell notes.

The layout at a glance

CUE COLUMN — about ⅓ of the width (2.5 in / 6.5 cm)

Questions & keywords, added after the lesson

NOTES AREA — about ⅔ of the width

The lesson itself, captured live in short phrases

SUMMARY BOX — full width, about 2 in / 5 cm tall

The whole page retold in your own words, within 24 hours

Above both columns sits a one-line header — subject, date and topic — which is what makes a notebook findable a month later. Every download on our template page follows these proportions exactly.

The four parts, with measurements

PartSizeWhat goes there
Header lineTop strip, one lineSubject · date · topic — written before the lesson starts
Cue columnLeft, ~30–35% of width (2.5 in / 6–6.5 cm)Questions and keywords that the notes beside them answer; written after class
Notes areaRight, the remaining ~⅔Notes in short phrases during the lesson, one idea per line, gaps between topics
Summary boxBottom, full width, ~2 in (5 cm) tallA 2–4 sentence summary of the whole page, in your own words, same day

Setting it up on paper

On ruled notebook paper (Letter or A4) you need two lines. Draw a vertical line about 6.5 cm (2.5 inches) from the left edge — on standard ruled paper that lands near the red margin line many notebooks already print, which is no coincidence: the format was designed around ordinary school paper. Then draw a horizontal line about 5 cm (2 inches) above the bottom edge. Write the header on the top line, and the page is ready. Leave the cue column empty during the lesson; it is filled in afterwards, which our step-by-step guide walks through.

Setting it up in Word or Google Docs

Digitally, the format is one table: insert a 2-column table, set the left column to roughly one third of the width, then add a second row merged across both columns for the summary. Set the header as a normal line of text above the table. In Google Docs: Insert → Table (2×1), drag the column divider left until the split looks like a third/two-thirds, then add the merged summary row beneath. If you would rather skip the setup, the ready-made Word and Google Docs templates are free to download.

Format mistakes that quietly break the method

MistakeWhy it hurts
Cue column drawn too wideNotes get squeezed; you write less in class and invent cues that fit nothing
Summary squeezed into one lineA one-line "summary" is a label, not a retelling — the memory benefit lives in the retelling
Notes written inside the cue columnCovering the left side no longer hides the answers, so self-testing stops working
No header lineWeeks later you cannot tell which lesson a page belongs to, and the notebook stops being a system
No gaps between topicsCues cannot line up with their notes, and the page turns back into an ordinary wall of text

Frequently asked questions

What are the measurements of the Cornell notes format?

The cue column takes about a third of the page width — roughly 2.5 inches (6.5 cm) on Letter or A4 — and the summary box runs the full width at the bottom, about 2 inches (5 cm) tall. The header is a single line at the top.

Is the cue column on the left or the right?

On the left, in the standard layout Walter Pauk designed. The left placement is what makes the revision trick work: cover the wide notes side with a sheet of paper and answer the cues you can still see.

Does the Cornell format work on A4 paper?

Yes — the proportions, not the paper size, define the format. Our template page offers both Letter and A4 versions with the same one-third/two-thirds split.
