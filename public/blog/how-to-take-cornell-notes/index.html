How to Take Cornell Notes: Step by Step

StudyAI Guides · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Taking Cornell notes is a routine, not a talent: set the page up once, record the lesson in the wide column, then — the part most students skip — spend ten minutes the same day turning those notes into questions and a summary. Those ten minutes are what turn a page of notes into something you can actually revise from. Here is the whole routine, stage by stage. New to the idea itself? Start with what Cornell notes are.

Stage 0: Set up the page

Divide the page before the lesson starts. Draw a vertical line about 2.5 inches (6 cm) from the left edge for the cue column, and a horizontal line about 2 inches (5 cm) from the bottom for the summary. Write the topic, class and date at the top, plus the one question this lesson should answer for you. Or skip the ruler entirely and print a stack of the free Cornell notes template (PDF for printing, Word/Google Docs for typing).

Stage 1: Record — during the lesson

Write only in the wide right-hand section, and write less than you want to. Capture main ideas, definitions, dates, diagrams and examples in short phrases — not the teacher's sentences word for word. Leave a blank line or two between ideas: you will thank yourself at Stage 2, when there is room to add what you missed. If something is unclear, mark it with a "?" in the margin instead of falling behind; unclear points become cues later.

Stage 2: Reduce — the same day, within 24 hours

This is the stage that does the learning. Re-read your notes and, in the left column, write a cue for each block of notes — a keyword or, better, a question whose answer is in the notes beside it. Turn statements into questions:

Your note saysThe cue becomes
"Mitochondria produce ATP, the cell's energy currency""Which organelle produces ATP — and what is ATP for?"
"The treaty was signed in 1919 and redrew the map of Europe""When was the treaty signed, and what did it change?"
"To differentiate x³, multiply by the power and reduce it by one""How do you differentiate x³? What is the general rule?"

Fill the gaps you left, correct anything wrong, and chase the "?" marks now — from the textbook, a classmate, or the teacher — while the lesson is still fresh.

Stage 3: Summarise — before you close the page

In the bottom strip, write two to four sentences that cover the whole page in your own words, without looking back at the notes first. If you cannot, the page is telling you something: that topic needs another pass today, not during exam week. A good test: your summary should make sense to a friend who missed the lesson.

Stage 4: Recite and review — where the marks come from

To revise, cover the right-hand column and answer each cue out loud or on scrap paper. Uncover, check, and mark the ones you missed — those get revisited first next time. Repeat on a spaced schedule (tomorrow, in three days, in a week) instead of cramming the whole notebook the night before. This recite step is active recall, and it is the reason the Cornell layout outperforms plain notes: the page was designed for it from the start. How to do Cornell notes on Google Docs or Word

The routine is identical on a laptop; only the setup differs. The fastest route is the Word template: download it, upload it to Google Drive, open it with Google Docs, then File → Make a copy for each new lesson so your blank master stays clean. Building it by hand works too: insert a 2-column table (narrow left, wide right) for cues and notes, add a one-row table underneath for the summary, and delete the table borders you don't want. On a tablet, import the PDF template into your notes app and write over it.

Adapting the routine

Maths and problem subjects: notes side = worked examples; cues = the steps and the "when do I use this?" triggers. Essay subjects: cues as essay-style questions ("To what extent was…?") make revision double as essay planning. Reading a textbook: one page per section, and write the essential question at the top before you start reading. Fast lectures: record sparser notes and lean harder on Stage 2 — the same-day pass is where understanding is built anyway.

Frequently asked questions

How long does one Cornell page take?

The lesson fills the notes column in real time. Budget about ten extra minutes per page afterwards: five for cues, two for the summary, and a few short recite rounds spread over the following week.

One page per lecture, or per topic?

Per topic or session is the practical rule — a long lecture may run to several pages, and that is fine. What matters is that every page gets its own cues and summary.

Should Cornell notes be handwritten or typed?

Both work. Handwriting forces you to select and rephrase, which many students find sticks better; typing is faster and neater for revision. The cues-and-summary routine matters far more than the tool.

What is the most common mistake beginners make?

Skipping Stage 2. Notes with an empty cue column are just regular notes, and regular notes are what you were trying to improve on.
