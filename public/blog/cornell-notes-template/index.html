What the page looks like

CUES / QUESTIONS

Keywords and self-test questions, added after the lesson, lined up with the notes they belong to.

NOTES

The wide area for the lesson itself: short sentences, diagrams and examples, with space left between ideas so you can add to them later.

SUMMARY

Two to four sentences at the bottom that condense the whole page in your own words.

Three habits that make the sheet work

Write the cues the same day. The cue column is where the learning happens — questions written a week later are just copying. Summarise before you close the notebook. Four honest sentences beat a perfect page you never review. Revise by covering the right side. Answer the cues out loud, then uncover and check — that is active recall, and repeating it on a spaced schedule is what moves the material into long-term memory.

Frequently asked questions

Is this Cornell notes template really free?

Yes — all three files are free to download and print as often as you like. There is no account, no email form and no watermark on the sheet.

Should I download the Letter or the A4 version?

The layout is identical; only the paper size differs. Letter is the standard in the US and Canada, A4 almost everywhere else. Pick the size your printer is loaded with, or the Word file if you are not printing at all.

How do I get this template into Google Docs?

Download the Word (.docx) file, upload it to Google Drive, open it with Google Docs, then use File → Make a copy. Keep the original blank and start each new copy for a new topic.

Can I type directly on the PDF?

The PDF is drawn for printing and handwriting, so for typing the Word file is the better tool — it opens in Word, Google Docs, LibreOffice and Pages. If you annotate PDFs on a tablet, import the PDF into your notes app instead.

Who created the Cornell note-taking method?

Walter Pauk, an education professor at Cornell University, developed the system in the 1950s so students would review and question their notes instead of only recording them. The template above follows his original three-part layout.
